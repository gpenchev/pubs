# Overall framework — brief

We construct a novel territorial threat measure, identify the structural
drivers of defence expenditure, use fixed effects to isolate genuine
within-country and within-year variation, test the spatial structure of
interdependence across multiple weight specifications, and examine whether
these relationships hold uniformly across time or are conditional on
geopolitical regime — finding that fiscal constraints dominate universally
while threat response is historically conditional.

---

## Three clarifications on the framework

**Fixed effects clean before variables explain.**
Country and year fixed effects are not found from the structural
variables — they are removed before the structural variables are tested.
Country effects absorb everything permanently different about each
country. Year effects absorb everything common to all countries in a
given year. Only after stripping those out do threat and fiscal variables
compete to explain what remains.

**The temporal dimension is central, not supplementary.**
The regime structure and structural break analysis are not robustness
checks. They are central to the argument. Without the temporal dimension
the paper is a standard determinants study. With it, it becomes a paper
about when and why the usual rules break down — fiscal constraint holds
universally, threat response activates only after extreme shocks.

**Spatial tests are about ruling out false patterns.**
The multiple spatial specifications do not add new explanatory variables.
They test whether the apparent similarity between neighbouring countries
reflects genuine strategic interaction or simply shared inertia. Finding
that the spatial lag disappears after controlling for persistence is not
a negative result — it is the result. It confirms that defence budgets
are driven by internal constraints, not by watching neighbours.
